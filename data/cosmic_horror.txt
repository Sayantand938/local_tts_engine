INCIDENT REPORT: THE HOLLOWAY STATION EVENT

At 3:14 on the morning of November ninth, the Holloway Deep Space Listening Station stopped answering its radio. Operators in the mountain valley below first assumed a storm had taken the antenna. By dawn, they knew the truth was stranger. The sky above the station was perfectly clear.

A recovery team of four reached the facility at noon. The main door was unlocked, and every light was on. Eleven staff members had been on duty that night. None were inside. Their coats still hung on the hooks. Their coffee was still warm.

The station's log was intact. According to the final entries, at 2:51 a.m. the dish had detected a signal from a region of space that astronomers had long called empty. The first technician, Dr. Marian Voss, wrote that the signal was not a pattern, but a pressure. She described it as a sound that arrived before it was heard.

By 3:02, the entries became shorter. Staff reported that the stars on the monitors had begun to rearrange themselves, drifting slowly into rows. One technician noted that the rows looked like teeth. Another wrote only one sentence, repeated forty times: it knows we are counting.

At 3:09, the cameras recorded something unusual. Every person in the building walked calmly to the observation deck and stood facing the window. Nobody spoke. Nobody ran. The recording shows them lifting their hands toward the sky, as if answering a question.

At 3:14, the footage ends. The final frame shows the empty deck, and the window, and a sky that, for one frame, contains no stars at all.

Investigators later made two discoveries. First, the dish had been rotated overnight to point at the ground, directly beneath the station. Second, beneath the building, engineers found a circular shaft that appears on no construction record. It descends further than their instruments can measure. Sounds from the shaft have been described by rescuers as breathing, slow and patient, with a rhythm that matched the heartbeat of whoever stood closest.

Two members of the recovery team have since refused to discuss what they heard. A third was found on the second night standing on the observation deck, facing the window, with his hands raised. He has not spoken since.

Authorities have sealed the valley and closed the road. Officials describe the case as an unexplained evacuation, and state that there is no danger to the public. Residents of the nearby town, however, report that their dogs refuse to face the mountain, and that at night, when the wind stops, they can hear a faint sound beneath the ground. It is steady and unhurried.

It sounds, they say, like something learning to count.

The investigation is ongoing. The station's final recording is classified. Anyone who has experienced unusual sounds, lights, or the sudden urge to look upward is asked to remain indoors and contact local authorities.
